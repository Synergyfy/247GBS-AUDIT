# Excess Stock Audit Framework
## Sector 3: Manufacturing & Industrial

This document defines the **Excess Stock Audit Question Framework** for the Manufacturing & Industrial sector.

It focuses on **raw material overstocking, work-in-progress accumulation, finished goods obsolescence, production waste, rejected batches, and storage inefficiencies**.

It includes:
- Short Form Trigger Questions
- Long Form Deep-Dive Questions
- Calculation Logic per Subcategory

---

## SECTOR: Manufacturing & Industrial

### Categories Covered
1. Light Manufacturing
2. Heavy Manufacturing
3. Food & Beverage Manufacturing
4. Chemical & Materials
5. Engineering & Fabrication

---

# 1. Light Manufacturing

## Subcategories
- Clothing Manufacturing
- Furniture Manufacturing
- Packaging Production
- Printing Presses

---

## Short Form Trigger Questions (Light Manufacturing)

1. What percentage of raw materials expires or degrades monthly?
2. How much finished stock remains unsold after 90 days?
3. Do minimum production runs create surplus?
4. What value of rejected batches is written off?
5. How often are materials damaged in storage?
6. Do design changes create obsolete stock?
7. What percentage of work-in-progress is idle?
8. How much packaging material is unused?
9. Do cancelled orders cause excess output?
10. Do you track ageing inventory?

---

## Long Form Deep-Dive Questions (Light Manufacturing)

1. Raw material wastage value (£)?
2. Finished goods ageing profile?
3. Overproduction rate?
4. Design revision write-offs?
5. Storage damage losses?
6. WIP stagnation cost?
7. Batch rejection frequency?
8. Forecast variance impact?
9. Supplier minimum order effect?
10. Obsolete SKU percentage?
11. Rework cost vs disposal cost?
12. Packaging overstock value?
13. Contract overrun losses?
14. Product lifecycle tracking?
15. Inventory turnover rate?

---

## Calculation Logic (Light Manufacturing)

Total Excess =
Raw Material Waste + Unsold Finished Goods + Obsolete Stock + Rejected Batches + Idle WIP

Excess % =
(Total Excess ÷ Total Production Input Cost) × 100
